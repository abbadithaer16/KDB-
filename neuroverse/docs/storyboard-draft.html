NEUROVERSE storyboard

Early visual storyboard for Checkpoint 2 (revision 2): five scenes, camera, positioning, lighting, type, transitions and Explore Mode.

These frames are schematic sketches. The brain and neuron shapes are placeholders drawn by hand, not the 3D models, and not anatomically accurate. Fonts fall back to system faces here. Labels mark topics; no scientific facts are stated until they are checked against sources.

Palette on tissue, dark and light

Tissue colours are realistic; violet and amber are illustrative and labelled as such on screen. Selection never relies on colour alone.

1 · Taking Shape

Introduce the brain as a real, measurable object, built from its own cross-sections. Construction, not a light reveal.

Camera
1a: vertical rise through the slice stack, then a slow pull-back and 30° orbit (about 6 s, presenter can skip). 1b: locked, anterolateral three-quarter view, about 28° field of view.
Object
Whole brain, right of centre, about 70% of frame height. Slices are computed by cutting the actual model, not invented.
Lighting
Neutral cool-white key from upper left, low fill, thin separation rim. Violet exists only in the contour lines and fades as tissue appears.
Type
Newsreader title, one Atkinson statement (≤ 25 words), view name, scale bar, step counter.
Transition in
Opening sequence starts on the presenter's first input, not automatically.
Explore
Not available (overview scene).
Science
Scientific: overall shape and orientation. Artistic: violet contours, labelled "Computed from the 3D model".

2 · The Architecture of Thought

The major structures and what they do, then a cut on the midline to show what lies inside.

Camera
In: orbit from scene 1's three-quarter view to the left lateral view (about 3 s). 2a locked. 2a→2b: orbit to the medial side while the section plane sweeps in (about 3.5 s), then locked.
Object
Brain left of centre in 2a (labels right), right of centre in 2b (labels left), so the composition changes sides with the reveal.
Lighting
Lower, more raking key than scene 1 to model the gyri and sulci; white matter in the cut is lit flatter so it reads as a cut surface.
Type
Title, up to 6 labels, view name, short hint for the section control.
Explore
Orbit (limited to the upper hemisphere of views), zoom within limits, tap or Tab to select a structure (dims the rest, violet edge, margin description), section plane control, reset (R), exit (Esc) returns to 2a or 2b exactly.
Science
Scientific: lobe boundaries, structure positions and names, functions at textbook level. Artistic: selection colour. Open question: which structures exist as separate meshes in the verified asset.

3 · The Neural Network

How a neuron signals: three beats in one step, advanced by the same next control.

Camera
In: from scene 2's view, the camera descends toward the cortex surface (real move, shallow depth of field), then a cross-exposure cut to the neuron (about 4 s). 3a locked, wide. 3b: low travelling shot alongside the axon during the signal (about 3 s), then locked. 3c: cross-exposure to the synapse, slow push-in, locked.
Object
3a: neuron on a diagonal from upper left (dendrites) to lower right (terminals), using depth: dendrites nearer, axon receding. 3c: terminal above, next neuron below, gap at the optical centre.
Lighting
Soft top key on membranes, deep black surround, no fog. The signal is the only light source that moves. Amber appears only at release.
Type
One heading per beat, short explanation, up to 5 labels, artistic disclaimers, data credit.
Explore
Limited orbit around the neuron in 3a (±40°), zoom limits, reset. No selection.
Science
Scientific: real morphology, direction of signal, order of synaptic events, structure names. Artistic: glow, colours, slowed timing, synapse geometry (simplified), scale cut. All marked on screen.

4 · Memory & Emotion

Memory and emotion as networks of structures working together, not single centres.

Camera
In: cross-exposure from the synapse back to the whole brain, then a slow arc from posterior to left oblique while the cortex fades to translucent (about 4 s). Locked for teaching.
Object
Brain right of centre, labels left; deep structures at the optical centre of the brain, visible through the cortex.
Lighting
Key from behind and above to give the translucent cortex a rim; deep structures lit from the front so they read solid.
Type
Title, one statement, 3–5 labels, disclaimers.
Explore
Orbit, zoom, select a structure to see its role (dims the rest), toggle connection paths, reset, exit restores the frame.
Science
Scientific: structure positions and roles as networks. Artistic: translucency, path colour, simplified routes (labelled "Simplified pathways"). Paths only where a real anatomical connection is sourced.

5 · The Human Mind

The synthesis: the one light scene. Exposure rises, and the brain becomes a drawing where everything from the journey is legible at once.

Camera
In: the ground rises from charcoal to plate while the camera pulls back and arcs to a calm lateral view (about 4 s). Locked.
Object
Brain right of centre, larger margin on the left for the synthesis labels; the scene 1 contours return as line work, closing the loop.
Lighting
No dramatic light: a lit plate. The 3D model is drawn as outline and contour lines (rendered from the real geometry, not a flat image).
Type
Title, one closing statement, three synthesis labels, credits access.
Explore
Not available; the final scene is a summary. A sources and credits panel opens from here.
Science
Scientific: the same geometry as before. Artistic: line rendering, violet thread.

Mobile and projector notes

Portrait screens: the margin column moves below the subject, labels become a list below the model where tapping a name highlights its structure, and teaching frames use their own camera framings. Projectors (1920×1080): all text at least 22 px at that size, no meaningful detail below the darkest tissue tones, and labels never placed over the subject.
